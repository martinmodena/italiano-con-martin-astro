Italian diminutives and augmentatives: -ino, -one, -accio

How they are formed: word + suffix

Drop the final vowel and add a suffix: gatt-o → gatt-ino (kitten). Where English adds a word (little, big, nasty), Italian changes the word itself: small (diminutive), dear (term of endearment), big (augmentative) or nasty (pejorative). The gender usually stays the same and the plural is regular: la casetta, le casette.

Base wordDiminutiveAugmentativePejorative
la casala casettala casonala casaccia
il libroil librettoil libroneil libraccio
il gattoil gattinoil gattoneil gattaccio
il ragazzoil ragazzinoil ragazzoneil ragazzaccio
la parolala parolinala parolonala parolaccia

Not every word takes every suffix: you learn them by using them. When in doubt, piccolo and grande always work: una piccola casa, un grande libro.

They can be stacked: casa → casetta → casettina, piccolo → piccolino. More suffixes, more affection.

Diminutives and terms of endearment

The diminutive says that something is small, but it often adds affection or politeness too, like English -ie in doggie or -let in booklet — only much more common. Italians use it with children, in the family and to ask for something nicely.

-ino / -ina: the most common
il gattino · il tavolino · la mammina · un momentino
-etto / -etta
la casetta · il libretto · la borsetta · la camicetta
-ello / -ella
l’alberello · il venticello · il paesello
-icino / -olino
il cuoricino · il libricino · il topolino
-uccio / -uccia: with affection
il tesoruccio · la casuccia · la boccuccia
Adjectives and adverbs too
carino · piccolino · benino · pochino · pianino

Un momentino, un caffettino, un attimino: here the diminutive doesn’t mean “small”, it makes the sentence friendlier, like “just a sec”. First names use it too: Paola → Paoletta, Giovanni → Giovannino, like Johnny.

Augmentatives: -one

The suffix -one (-ona in the feminine) says that something is big or chunky. English needs a word (a huge book); Italian just says un librone. With people it can be friendly or ironic.

-one: big, chunky
un librone · un nasone · un omone · un ragazzone
Feminine nouns often become masculine
la porta → il portone · la finestra → il finestrone · la febbre → il febbrone
From verbs: someone who does it too much
mangiare → un mangione · chiacchierare → una chiacchierona · brontolare → un brontolone

Watch the gender: many feminine nouns become masculine with -one: la porta → il portone (the main door of a building). And uomo becomes omone, without the u.

Pejoratives: -accio, -astro

The suffix -accio (-accia) says that something is bad or nasty; -astro says that a person isn’t worth much, or that a colour is “-ish”, as in yellowish.

-accio: bad, nasty
che tempaccio! · una giornataccia · le parolacce · un ragazzaccio
-astro: of little value
un giovinastro · un poetastro
-astro with colours: “-ish”
giallastro · verdastro · rossastro · dolciastro
In the family: no judgement
il fratellastro · la sorellastra

Fratellastro and sorellastra are not bad siblings: they are half-brothers and half-sisters, or stepbrothers and stepsisters. Le parolacce are swear words: dire le parolacce = to swear.

Italian altered nouns compared with English

English has few suffixes of this kind (-ie in doggie, -let in booklet, -ette in kitchenette) and mostly uses adjectives: little, big, nasty. Italian changes the word itself.

EnglishItalian
a kitten, a little catun gattino
a little houseuna casetta
a huge bookun librone
awful weatherun tempaccio
just a sec!un momentino!
a half-brother, a stepbrotherun fratellastro
  • English speakers tend to say un piccolo gatto: it is correct, but un gattino sounds much more natural.
  • -ish with colours is -astro: yellowish → giallastro.
  • Swear words are le parolacce; i paroloni are long, pompous words.

False diminutives

Some words look altered, but they aren’t. Others used to be, but today they are new words with a meaning of their own.

They look like diminutives, but aren’t
il mulino · il tacchino · il postino
They look like augmentatives, but aren’t
il mattone · il bottone · il burrone · il lampone · il limone
Altered words that became new words
il panino · gli spaghetti · l’ombrellone · il cassetto · il rossetto · la cartolina · il fazzoletto

Il mulino (mill) is not a little mule, and il mattone (brick) is not a big madman. Il panino is not just any small bread: it is the Italian sandwich. L’ombrellone is a beach umbrella, il rossetto is lipstick, and gli spaghetti come from spago, “string”.

Typical mistakes English speakers make

Wrong: La portona è chiusa. Correct: Il portone è chiuso.
With -one many feminine nouns become masculine: il portone.
Wrong: Non dire parolone! Correct: Non dire parolacce!
Swear words are parolacce; paroloni are pompous words.
Wrong: Quel signore è un uomone. Correct: Quel signore è un omone.
Uomo + -one loses the u: omone.
