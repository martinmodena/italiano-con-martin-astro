When do we use it?

Kind requests
Mi diresti il tuo nome?
Wishes
Vorrei parlare meglio italiano.
Advice
Dovresti leggere ogni giorno.
Light hypotheses
Io andrei in treno, non in auto.

Very useful: when you book something, ask for information or want to sound polite, the condizionale changes the tone straight away.

The conditional compared with English

English puts would, could, should before the verb. Italian packs it all into one word: I would go → andrei, could you → potresti, you should → dovresti.

EnglishItalian
I'd like a coffee.Vorrei un caffè.
Could you help me?Potresti aiutarmi?
You should sleep more.Dovresti dormire di più.
If I had time, I would travel.Se avessi tempo, viaggerei.
Every summer we would go to the sea.Ogni estate andavamo al mare.
  • After se (if) Italian uses the subjunctive, not the conditional: se avessi tempo, not se avrei tempo.
  • Would for past habits is not the conditional: andavamo (imperfetto).
  • I'd like → vorrei is the most natural way to ask in a bar or a shop.

Typical mistakes English speakers make

Wrong: Se avrei tempo, viaggerei. Correct: Se avessi tempo, viaggerei.
If I had: after se you need the subjunctive.
Wrong: Da bambino andrei al mare ogni estate. Correct: Da bambino andavo al mare ogni estate.
Would for a past habit → imperfetto.
Wrong: Io vorrebbe un caffè. Correct: Io vorrei un caffè.
With io the ending is -ei.
