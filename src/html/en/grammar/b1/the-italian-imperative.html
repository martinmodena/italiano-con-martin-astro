Italian imperative

Give instructions, advice and invitations with every person: ascolta (tu), andiamo (noi), entrate (voi), si accomodi (Lei). Plus a trick to remember the forms effortlessly.

When is it used?

We use the imperative for instructions, invitations, advice and direct requests. The tone depends a lot on the situation.

Instruction
Gira a destra.
Invitation
Entra, per favore.
Advice
Dormi un po'.
Prohibition
Non parlare adesso.

The imperative compared with English

English has one imperative for one person or many: speak! Italian has two: parla (one person, tu) and parlate (several, voi). And the negative with tu uses the infinitive, not the imperative.

EnglishItalian
Speak more slowly! (one person)Parla più lentamente!
Speak more slowly! (two or more)Parlate più lentamente!
Don't speak now!Non parlare adesso!
Take it!Prendilo!
Listen to me!Ascoltami!
  • Don't + verb with tu is non + infinitive: non parlare, non prendere. With voi the normal form stays: non parlate.
  • Take it, tell me: the pronoun is glued to the end of the verb, as one word: prendilo, dimmi.
  • Tone comes from your voice and per favore: the Italian imperative isn't rude. Entra, per favore is a friendly invitation.

The five persons: tu, noi, voi, Lei, Loro

The Italian imperative has five persons. There is no io: we don't give orders to ourselves.

InfinitiveTuNoiVoiLeiLoro

prendereprendiprendiamoprendeteprendaprendano
partirepartipartiamopartitepartapartano
finirefiniscifiniamofinitefiniscafiniscano
  • Noi is English let's: Andiamo! (let's go!), Mangiamo qualcosa! (let's eat something!).
  • Lei is the polite form for one person you address formally: Signora, entri pure. (please come in, madam).
  • Loro is the polite form for several people. It's rare today (waiters, announcements, letters): in everyday life people use voi.
  • Negative: with tu, non + infinitive (non parlare); with every other person, non + the same form: non parliamo, non parlate, non parli, non parlino.

The trick to remember: two families

Tu, noi, voi → same as the present
These are the people you talk to as equals, your "friends". Prendi = tu prendi, parti = tu parti, parliamo = noi parliamo, parlate = voi parlate. Only one exception: tu with -are verbs, which keep their a: parlare → parla!
Lei, Loro → same as the subjunctive
These are the polite persons, the "ladies and gentlemen". Think of a sentence with che, then drop the che: (che) Lei entri → Entri! · (che) Loro aspettino → Aspettino!

Tu and Lei swap their vowels

-are verbs-ere / -ire verbs
tuparlaprendi, parti
Leiparliprenda, parta

The a and the i change places: tu parla ↔ Lei parli, tu prendi ↔ Lei prenda.

Remember: present tense among friends, subjunctive for ladies and gentlemen. Pronouns follow the same two families: with tu, noi, voi they're glued to the end (prendilo, prendiamolo, prendetelo); with Lei and Loro they go before (lo prenda, lo prendano).

For English speakers: let's isn't a separate word in Italian, it's the noi form: Let's go! = Andiamo! And English has no polite "you": in Italian, politeness lives in the verb, Entri! instead of Entra!

Irregular verbs, the easy way

The trick works here too: Lei and Loro take the subjunctive (vada, faccia, dica, venga…), noi and voi are almost always the present. There are only a few surprises:

  • The five short forms with tu: va', da', fa', sta', di'. Say them like a nursery rhyme. For the first four the present works too: vai, dai, fai, stai.
  • Add a pronoun and the consonant doubles: di' + mi = dimmi, fa' + lo = fallo, da' + mi = dammi, va' + ci = vacci. Only exception: gli (digli, fagli).
  • Essere, avere, sapere are the only ones with a surprise for tu and voi too: sii, abbi, sappi; siate, abbiate, sappiate (from the subjunctive). Sii paziente! (be patient!)

With pronouns

With tu, noi, voi the pronoun is attached to the end; with Lei and Loro it goes before the verb.

Prendi il libro. Prendilo.
Ascolta me. Ascoltami.
Mandate la foto. Mandatela.
Il treno parte: prendiamolo!
Signora, il modulo: lo firmi qui.
Signori, si accomodino.
Non lo prendere. / Non prenderlo.
Signora, non si preoccupi.

Typical mistakes English speakers make

Wrong: Non parla adesso! Correct: Non parlare adesso!
Don't speak with tu is non + infinitive.
Wrong: Lo prendi! Correct: Prendilo!
Take it: the pronoun is attached to the end of the imperative.
Wrong: Non fai rumore! Correct: Non fare rumore!
Don't make noise: again non + infinitive.
Wrong: Tu parla italiano! Correct: Parla italiano!
Just like in English, the imperative has no subject.
