The Italian imperfetto tense: giocavo, ero, facevo

Da bambino giocavo a calcio, mio nonno era alto, faceva freddo, mentre leggevo è arrivato Marco: the imperfetto tells habits (“I used to”), describes and sets the scene. Its uses, the form (parlavo, prendevo, dormivo), the irregular verbs (ero, facevo, dicevo, bevevo), signal words, how it works with the passato prossimo in a story, and the traps. With 30 exercises.

When to use it

The imperfetto is the tense of memories and descriptions: how things were, what people used to do, what was happening at a moment in the past. English splits these ideas between “used to”, “would”, “was … -ing” and the simple past; Italian puts them all in one tense.

A past habit (“used to”)
Da bambino giocavo a calcio ogni giorno.
Describing people and things
Mio nonno era alto e aveva i capelli bianchi.
Time, weather, age
Erano le otto, faceva freddo e avevo dieci anni.
An action in progress, interrupted (“was … -ing”)
Leggevo quando è arrivato Marco.
Two actions at the same time
Mentre cucinavo, ascoltavo la radio.
A polite request
Buongiorno, volevo un caffè.

A useful question: “what was it like?” or “what used to happen?” → imperfetto; “what happened?” → passato prossimo. And volevo at the bar or in a shop is a polite way to ask for something now, like “I wanted / I was wondering”: volevo due biglietti.

The imperfetto compared with English

English has no single tense for the imperfetto: it uses used to, would, was … -ing or the simple past. In Italian all these ideas (habits, descriptions, actions in progress) become the imperfetto.

EnglishItalian
When I was a child, I used to play football.Da bambino giocavo a calcio.
I was reading when Marco arrived.Leggevo quando è arrivato Marco.
It was cold and it was raining.Faceva freddo e pioveva.
Every summer we would go to the sea.Ogni estate andavamo al mare.
  • Used to and would (habits) aren't translated word for word: the imperfetto is enough, giocavo, andavamo.
  • Was reading → leggevo (or stavo leggendo). The action that interrupts goes in the passato prossimo: quando è arrivato Marco.
  • The simple past splits in two: a finished event → passato prossimo; a description or habit → imperfetto.

The form

It is the most regular tense in Italian: remove -re from the infinitive and add -vo, -vi, -va, -vamo, -vate, -vano. The vowel before it (a, e, i) stays the same as in the infinitive.

parla-re → parla-vo, prende-re → prende-vo, dormi-re → dormi-vo. -isc- verbs don’t use it: finivo, capivo. Watch the stress with loro: parlàvano, prendèvano, on the third-last syllable.

Irregular verbs

Very few. Essere has its own forms; fare, dire, bere use an old stem (fac-, dic-, bev-) with the usual endings.

Verbs that are irregular in the present, like avere, andare, stare, venire, uscire, are regular in the imperfetto: avevo, andavo, stavo, venivo, uscivo. The same goes for verbs like tradurre (traducevo) and porre (ponevo).

Signal words

Some words often come with the imperfetto, because they talk about habits or long periods.

Habits
sempre · di solito · spesso · ogni giorno · ogni estate
Periods of life
da bambino · da giovane · una volta · a quei tempi
Mentre (while): two things at the same time
Mentre studiavo, mio fratello giocava.

Mentre almost always takes the imperfetto, because it describes something that lasts. But careful: a signal word helps, it doesn’t decide on its own. Ieri (yesterday) works with both tenses: ieri pioveva (description), ieri ho visto Anna (event).

In a story

When you tell a story, the imperfetto and the passato prossimo work together, like the set and the actors in a film.

Imperfetto: the background, the scene
Era sabato, pioveva e io ero a casa.
Passato prossimo: the event, what happens
Alle sei ha suonato il telefono.
An event interrupts an action in progress
Mentre guardavo un film, è andata via la luce.

The imperfetto describes, the passato prossimo moves the story forward. If a long action is interrupted by a short one, the long one goes into the imperfetto (English “was … -ing”) and the short one into the passato prossimo (English simple past). The traps

Four points where many learners go wrong.

Io ero, lui era
io ero · lui era
With a precise duration: passato prossimo
Abitavo a Roma. · Ho abitato a Roma per due anni.
Imperfetto or stavo + gerund
Leggevo. · Stavo leggendo.
Volevo: polite, even about now
Volevo un caffè. · Volevo chiederti una cosa.

“Used to” is never translated word for word: “I used to play” is just giocavo. Ero is “I was”, era is “he / she was”: the most common mistake. With a precise, finished duration Italian uses the passato prossimo: ho abitato a Roma per due anni, but abitavo a Roma. For an action in progress you can also use stavo + gerund, never ero + gerund: see stare + gerundio.

Typical mistakes English speakers make

Wrong: Usavo giocare a calcio. Correct: Giocavo a calcio.
Used to isn’t translated: the imperfetto is enough.
Wrong: Ero leggendo quando è arrivato Marco. Correct: Leggevo quando è arrivato Marco.
I was reading → leggevo or stavo leggendo, never ero + gerund.
Wrong: Da bambino ho giocato a calcio ogni giorno. Correct: Da bambino giocavo a calcio ogni giorno.
A habit (every day) needs the imperfetto, even where English uses the simple past.
Wrong: Io era stanco. Correct: Io ero stanco.
I was → ero; era is “he / she was”.
Wrong: Ieri è stato freddo. Correct: Ieri faceva freddo.
It was cold: a description, imperfetto, and weather with fare.
