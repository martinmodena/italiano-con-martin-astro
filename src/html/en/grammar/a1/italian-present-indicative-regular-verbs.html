Italian present indicative, regular verbs: parlo, prendo, dormo

When to use it

The present is the most used tense in Italian. It covers what happens now, habits, things that are always true and often the near future. One Italian form does the job of English “I speak”, “I’m speaking” and often “I’m going to speak”.

Now
Parlo con Maria al telefono.
A habit
La mattina prendo il caffè al bar.
Something always true
L’acqua bolle a cento gradi.
The near future
Domani parto per Roma.
How long: present + da
Studio italiano da due anni.

Domani parto (I’m leaving tomorrow): for fixed plans Italians almost always use the present, not the future. And for something that started in the past and is still going on, Italian uses the present with da, where English uses “have been”: abito qui da tre anni, “I have lived here for three years”.

The present compared with English

In English the verb only changes with he and she (speaks); Italian has six endings, so you don’t need the subject. And one Italian present covers both I speak and I’m speaking.

EnglishItalian
I speak Italian.Parlo italiano.
I'm reading a book.Leggo un libro.
Do you work here?Lavori qui?
I don't eat meat.Non mangio la carne.
I've lived here for two years.Abito qui da due anni.
  • No do / does: a question is just the sentence with a question mark, Lavori qui?
  • I’ve lived here for two years → present + da: abito qui da due anni.
  • Listen to, wait for, look for → ascoltare, aspettare, cercare, with no preposition.

The three conjugations

Italian verbs end in -are, -ere or -ire. Remove that part and add the ending for the person: parl-are → parl-o. English only adds -s for he and she; Italian has six endings.

Three things to remember. With io it’s always -o, with tu always -i, with noi always -iamo. Lei (formal “you”) works like lui and lei: Lei parla. With loro the stress moves back: PAR-la-no, PREN-do-no, not parlAno; see also the pronunciation lesson.

There is no rule that tells you which -ire verbs take -isc-: you learn them. The most common ones without -isc- are in the second box above. Watch the pronunciation: capisco and capiscono have the sk sound, capisci and capisce the sh sound of “ship”.

Spelling

Some verbs change their spelling a little so that the sound stays the same.

-care and -gare: an h before i
cercare: cerco, cerchi, cerchiamo · pagare: pago, paghi, paghiamo
-iare: only one i
mangiare: mangio, mangi, mangiamo · studiare: studio, studi, studiamo
-cere and -gere: same spelling, different sound
leggere: leggo, leggi · vincere: vinco, vinci

Cerchi with h sounds like cerco (k); without the h, cerci would sound like “chee”. The other way round, leggo has a hard g and leggi a soft one (as in “jeans”): the spelling is normal, only the sound changes. Mangiare and studiare never have two i’s: mangi, studi, mangiamo.

Subject, negatives, questions

Three simple rules to build sentences.

You usually leave out the subject
Parlo italiano. · Io parlo italiano, lui no.
Negative: non before the verb
Non mangio la carne. · Non lavoriamo il sabato.
Questions: same word order, different intonation
Parli italiano? · Dove abiti? · Che cosa prendete?
With Lei (formal): the form of lui and lei
Lei parla inglese? · Scusi, Lei abita qui?

The ending already tells you who is doing the action, so io, tu, noi are only used for emphasis or contrast: io lavoro, tu no! Italian has no “do” or “does”: “Do you speak Italian?” is just Parli italiano?, with your voice going up at the end; “I don’t eat” is non mangio. To say how often you do something, see the adverbs of frequency.

The traps

Four points where many learners go wrong.

Ascoltare, aspettare, cercare: no preposition
Ascolto la musica. · Aspetto l’autobus. · Cerco le chiavi.
Abitare a (city) and in (country)
Abito a Roma. · Abito in Italia.
The stress of loro
parlano · prendono · abitano
The most common verbs are irregular
essere, avere, fare, andare, stare, venire…

English says “listen to”, “wait for”, “look for”: Italian doesn’t, ascolto la musica, aspetto Luca, cerco un lavoro. You live a Roma but in Italia. And careful: the most frequent verbs (essere, avere, fare, andare…) don’t follow these rules; you’ll find them in the lesson on the present of irregular verbs, and verbs like alzarsi are in the lesson on reflexive verbs.

Typical mistakes English speakers make

Wrong: Io parla italiano. Correct: Io parlo italiano.
With io the ending is always -o.
Wrong: Fai lavori qui? Correct: Lavori qui?
Do you work: Italian has no do, the verb alone is enough.
Wrong: Ascolto alla musica. Correct: Ascolto la musica.
Listen to, but ascoltare takes no preposition.
Wrong: Abito qui per due anni. Correct: Abito qui da due anni.
For two years (and still now) → da.
Wrong: Tu cerci le chiavi. Correct: Tu cerchi le chiavi.
Verbs in -care need an h: cerchi.
