Gender and number of Italian nouns: il libro, i libri

Il libro, la casa, i libri, le case: in Italian every noun is masculine or feminine, and it changes in the plural. The vowel rule, the plural, masculine and feminine for people, nouns that don’t change (la città, il bar) and the exceptions (il problema, la mano, le uova). With 30 exercises.

Masculine or feminine?

In Italian every noun is masculine or feminine, things too: il libro (the book) is masculine, la casa (the house) is feminine. English has nothing like this, but the last letter almost always tells you.

-o: almost always masculine
il libro · il gatto · il treno
-a: almost always feminine
la casa · la pizza · la scuola
-e: masculine or feminine
il pane · il fiore · la chiave · la notte
Helpful endings: -zione, -tà feminine; -ore masculine
la stazione · la città · il colore · il dottore

Learn every noun with its article: not pane but il pane (bread); not chiave but la chiave (key). With nouns in -e it’s the only way to know the gender. Words in -zione are often like English “-tion”: la stazione, la lezione.

Gender and number compared with English

In English things have no gender (the book, the house) and the plural takes -s. In Italian every noun is masculine or feminine and the plural changes the last vowel.

EnglishItalian
the book → the booksil libro → i libri
the house → the housesla casa → le case
the key → the keysla chiave → le chiavi
two coffeesdue caffè
people are nicela gente è simpatica
  • The has four forms in the singular and three in the plural: il, lo, la, l’ → i, gli, le.
  • No -s: due libri; and English words used in Italian don’t change: i computer, not “i computers”.
  • La gente is singular, while people is plural.

The plural

In the plural the last vowel changes: no -s as in English. One book, two books → un libro, due libri.

SingularPluralExample
-o-iil libro → i libri
-a-ela casa → le case
-e-iil fiore → i fiori · la chiave → le chiavi
-ca · -ga-che · -ghel’amica → le amiche · la riga → le righe
-co · -go-ci · -chi · -ghil’amico → gli amici · il gioco → i giochi

The article changes too: il → i, lo → gli, la → le, l’ → gli (masculine) or le (feminine). All the forms are in the lesson on definite articles. With -ca, -ga an h is added to keep the hard sound: amica → amiche. With -co, -go usually too: gioco → giochi, but amico → amici, medico → medici.

People: masculine and feminine

For people (and many animals) there is often a masculine and a feminine form, like English “actor / actress”.

MasculineFeminine
il ragazzola ragazza
l’amicol’amica
il signorela signora
lo studentela studentessa
il dottorela dottoressa
l’attorel’attrice
il cantantela cantante
il turistala turista

-o → -a is the most common case: il ragazzo, la ragazza (the boy, the girl). Some nouns add -essa (la studentessa, la dottoressa) or -trice (l’attrice). Nouns in -ante and -ista stay the same: only the article changes, il cantante, la cantante (the singer).

Nouns that don’t change

Some nouns are the same in the singular and in the plural: only the article changes, like English “sheep”.

With a stress mark on the last vowel
la città → le città · il caffè → i caffè
Foreign words
il bar → i bar · lo sport → gli sport
Shortened words
la foto → le foto · la bici → le bici · il cinema → i cinema

How can you tell singular from plural? Look at the article: la città (one city), le città (many). English words used in Italian don’t take an -s: i computer, gli sport, never “i computers”. Shortened words keep the gender of the long word: la foto (la fotografia), la bici (la bicicletta), il cinema (il cinematografo).

The exceptions

A few words break the rule, but they are everyday words: it’s worth learning them straight away.

Masculine in -a: plural in -i
il problema → i problemi · il programma → i programmi
Feminine in -o
la mano → le mani · la radio → le radio
A completely different plural
l’uomo → gli uomini
Masculine in the singular, feminine in the plural
l’uovo → le uova · il braccio → le braccia
La gente: singular
la gente è simpatica · le persone sono simpatiche

Many masculine nouns in -a come from Greek and end in -ma, often like English words: il problema, il programma, il tema, il clima. Some body parts have a feminine plural in -a: le braccia, le dita, le ginocchia. And la gente (people) is singular, while English “people” is plural: la gente è simpatica, “people are nice”. Adjectives change with the noun too: see the lesson on adjectives.

Typical mistakes English speakers make

Wrong: i casa Correct: le case
Feminine plural: article le and ending -e.
Wrong: due libros Correct: due libri
No -s: -o becomes -i.
Wrong: la problema Correct: il problema
Problema is masculine, like the nouns in -ma.
Wrong: i computers Correct: i computer
English words used in Italian don’t change in the plural.
Wrong: la gente sono simpatiche Correct: la gente è simpatica
People is plural, la gente is singular.
